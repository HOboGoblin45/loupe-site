What independent fashion is actually doing

Nobody measures the independent tier. Enterprise forecasting starts above it, wholesale data stays private, and a brand's own dashboard shows only its own store. So we built the instrument out of the thing we already had: a full snapshot of 200 labels' catalogues, taken every day since 2026-06-17.

Some of the biggest "discounts" in this data were ours, and they are excluded. A real sale marks different pieces down by different amounts. A currency correction multiplies every piece by exactly the same number. We look for the second: a label whose whole line steps by one identical ratio on one day is treated as our pipeline changing, not the brand changing its mind, and prices are never compared across that day for that label. 60 such brand-days exist across the whole window and 45 of them moved by exactly a currency-pair ratio from our own conversion table — Stine Goya's line moved ×0.134, which is the Danish krone peg, not an 87% sale. 18 fall inside the window above:

Your own numbers against the tier

We build a private card for each of the 155 labels with enough tracked pieces to measure: your sell-out rate, your turnover, your refresh rate and where your prices sit in your own categories against everyone else's — each one next to the tier figure over the same window. It is free, it is yours, and nothing on it is published here — the card is stored encrypted and the link we send you is the only key to it.

Email tryloupeapp@gmail.com with your label's name and we will send the link.

How this is measured, and what it cannot tell you

What we see. Every label's public product feed, read once a day and committed. We keep up to 750 pieces per label, drawn from its 250 most recently published listings. For a small store that is the entire store; for a large one it is the front of the catalogue. Every number on this page is of what we track and we mean it literally.

Why 200 and not 232. The Loupe app carries 232 labels, but 32 of those reach it only through a partner boutique's shelf. What a boutique chooses to buy, and what it charges, is that boutique's decision — not the label's. Mixing the two would corrupt every number here, so the Index reads brand-direct storefronts only: labels publishing their own prices, their own assortment, their own drops.

The window. 60 daily snapshots, 2026-06-17 to 2026-08-19. Comparisons over time start at 2026-08-09, when our roster stopped growing — before that, new pieces are us adding labels, not brands publishing. The daily job did not run between 2026-07-24 and 2026-07-29, so 4 days are missing from the middle of the window.

Turnover is measured once, between two dates. Day-to-day disappearance is 45% noise in this data: of 10,059 overnight vanishings, 4,552 came back. Anything computed as a daily rate would be mostly measurement.

Our own decisions are removed. 4 labels left our roster in the window (De V, Designation, Gitika, MAS1), some by our own choice. Every longitudinal number here uses only the 224 labels present on both dates.

Prices are never compared across a change in how we read them. On 2026-07-15 we pinned our scrape to the US market and 49 geo-priced labels flipped to USD; the next day 36.5% of the catalogue moved price. That whole boundary is void here. Separately, any single label whose line moves by one identical multiplier on one day is voided for that label — 60 such brand-days were found, 45 of them landing on an exact currency-pair ratio.

Stock data starts 2026-07-16. Before that we did not record whether a piece was in stock, so nothing earlier is asked. Partner-boutique listings are excluded from sell-out entirely, because we only ingest those in stock.

What we cannot tell you. Units, revenue, margin, returns, or whether a sold-out piece sold out at full price. We see a catalogue, not a till. We also cannot see a brand's stock depth — "sold out" here means no size is purchasable, which for a made-to-order label may never happen however well it sells.

Would our own users' swipes predict any of this? No — and we checked. Across 133 labels, in-app approval rate against sell-out rate gives r = +0.09, which at this sample is indistinguishable from nothing. The Index does not use app behaviour anywhere.

The record. Every figure above is in data.json, regenerated on the same schedule and kept in version control, so any week's numbers can be diffed against the last.
